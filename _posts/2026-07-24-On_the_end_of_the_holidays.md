title: "On the End of the Holidays"
date: 2026-07-24
---

There is a particular sadness that arrives at the end of longer holidays. It has nothing to do with the place I am leaving, only with the self I am leaving behind, a version of myself the rest of the year rarely has room for. On the last days of holidays, if I simply stay in this moment, I feel contentment. It is only when I think about tomorrow that my mood darkens.

This feeling is not new for me. I grew up somewhere with ten week summers, and I remember other children counting down the days, eager to get back to school. I wasn't like them. Going back was not a joy for me, not even then.

What I mourn on the last day is a way of being: waking up and deciding, without justifying it to anyone. Learning because something is interesting, not because it will be tested. Noticing a detail in the street, an unplanned event, without an agenda shouting for my attention.

It isn’t effort I struggle with. It’s being told when and what: an hour fixed by someone else’s clock, a goal set by someone else’s measure. Under that, even things I love start to feel like performance. It usually takes a holiday longer than a week, before my mind loses connection to  its obligations: the things to do, the messages, the quiet arithmetic of will I have energy left for what’s actually mine? Eventually what’s left is simple: not what must I do, but what do I want to do, now.

This is the real holiday, the temporary *return of a self that gets to choose*. Which is why the last day is never just about going back to the office. It is a small, private goodbye to the *version of myself I only meet when no one else is setting the schedule*.
